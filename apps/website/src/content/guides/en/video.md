# From preview to finished video

## Start with what you need

Identify the timing, movement or sound you need to test and use the simplest sufficient material. Existing clips can be used directly; not every shot needs a 3D preview or a full generated asset set.

Start a video from an idea, a script, storyboards or existing clips. A video is an independent project. Connect a script or novel when useful, without creating empty upstream works.

## Describe the goal and the material you have

Tell your external Agent what you want to see:

> I have three clips of a confrontation at a doorway. Check character continuity and action, then prepare an edit with temporary sound. Let us hear footsteps inside before cutting to the door. Do not pay for new generation yet.

Your Agent inventories material, sources and missing pieces. It uses your chosen tools for boards, previews, video, sound and editing, then returns results and an editable project. You return to watch without having to operate a 3D scene, node graph or multitrack editor first.

## Use the preview that answers your question

| Question | Material to inspect first |
| --- | --- |
| Motivation, causality and dialogue | Complete script and any needed source comparison |
| Reveals, cuts, pauses and sound entrances | Animatic or an edit with temporary sound |
| Blocking, sightlines, occlusion and camera movement | A simple 3D preview when needed |
| Identity, acting, lip sync and final visual quality | Representative actual generated clips |

Ordinary dialogue does not require a 3D preview for every shot. Proxy colors and geometry explain space; they are not final character or environment requirements. A successful preview does not guarantee that a model will reproduce it exactly.

## Agree on content and spending before generation

Ask your Agent to explain the model and mode, character and location references, framing, duration, sound and spending limit. Check adaptation rights, permission to send material to an external service and permission to redistribute it separately.

Approve a clear scope in the current Agent conversation. Work can continue within it; decide again when scope or spending would exceed it. For an unknown result, check the original task and retain its billing record instead of creating a replacement call.

Hetu’s optional text and image services use Lime Cloud accounts and credits. Video and sound use external tools selected by your Agent; the text and image catalog does not establish video support.

## Watch continuously, then point to the change

Watch the full preview or edit with its sound and context. Pause anywhere, follow character appearances or inspect changes and questions. A few frames selected by the Agent are not the full work.

Feedback can be plain: “Hear the footsteps before the cut,” “This subtitle is late,” or “Keep the costume from the previous scene.” Let the Agent retain the version and interval before changing the project. Compare the new result with the old one; earlier feedback stays with the earlier version.

Story problems return to the script; staging problems to the preview; appearance problems to references; subtitle or mixing problems to the relevant text or audio. Local revisions preserve usable clips, earlier versions and billing records.

## Verify both the finished video and the project

Watch the complete picture, listen to dialogue, music and effects, and check subtitles, timing and continuity. A download link or a few stills cannot replace viewing. Successful playback cannot replace your creative judgment.

Preview approval, asset selection, clip selection, final-cut confirmation and project delivery are separate decisions. Deliver selected video, subtitles, audio and an editable project. Check source media, fonts, relative paths and other dependencies by actually opening them in the recipient’s environment.

See [Formats and delivery](formats-and-delivery.md). Publishing, promotion and subsequent operations are separate activities; confirming a cut does not start them.
