# Images and illustrations

## Start with what you need

Organize images needed for comparison, use or delivery. Discussing a direction need not generate a complete image set.

Character images, costume references, locations and story illustrations serve different tasks. Explain the purpose, have your Agent fix the object and references, then compare the actual images in Hetu.

You can prepare one important character image or a small set for a scene. You do not need a complete art library before beginning.

## Begin with a specific purpose

> Prepare a solo front-view image of the watchmaker using her current profile and chosen costume. Separately prepare a story illustration of her outside the shop, linked to the doorway passage in chapter one. Record the purposes separately. Show the plan and check costs before generating.

A character image answers “who is she?” An illustration answers “what happens here?” A character image group belongs to one person. Keep group scenes as illustrations rather than using them as solo identity references.

Covers have their own selection and history; see [Create and choose a cover](cover-art.md).

## Fix the references and what must remain

Ask the Agent to identify the character, costume, location and prop revisions used. Explain each reference's role: which establishes the face, which describes clothing and which guides composition.

When references conflict, decide first. Do not let the model silently combine different ages or outfits, or turn incidental objects into confirmed story facts.

Add side, back or expression views when the task needs them, preserving the same person and costume. See [Characters, locations and props](characters-and-scenes.md).

## View images with their objects and in the library

Find character images with the character and location images with the place. Story illustrations retain their chapter, scene or passage reference. The shared image library gives you another route to the same result, without duplicate copies named alike.

Previews preserve the image's proportions and can be enlarged. A cropped thumbnail is not enough to judge the full composition of a portrait or landscape image.

Before reviewing a candidate, identify its task and reference version. Receiving it does not mean selecting it.

## Make feedback specific

| What you notice | What you can say |
| --- | --- |
| A different person | “Keep the face and hair from the selected front view.” |
| Inconsistent clothing | “Only fix the left sleeve's damage; keep color and material.” |
| Incorrect layout | “The door is behind her and the clock remains on the right wall.” |
| A spoiler in the illustration | “Remove the father in the background; retain the light through the door.” |

Attach feedback to the current image or region. Compare the new result when it arrives; an old selection rectangle belongs to the old image rather than automatically applying to a new one.

After a local fix, check the whole picture too. Repairing a hand should not change identity, proportions or the intended action.

## Check selection and delivery

Keep prior candidates, sources and feedback. Before selecting new material, check its impact instead of automatically replacing assets already used elsewhere.

For a collaborator, verify the original files, purpose, rights and required references. A preview or expired download URL is not a complete asset handoff.
