# Characters, locations and props

## Start with what you need

Develop characters, locations and states as scenes need them. A temporary idea need not become a confirmed design or story fact.

Assets should answer concrete creative questions: who a person is, where a scene happens and which objects must stay consistent. Organize confirmed facts before preparing text and pictures.

Keep identity, costume, location and prop references clear about their purpose and source. That makes it easier to know which version to use during revisions.

## Start with the actual scenes

> Organize the people, costumes, location and props for the watch-shop scene in episode one. Reuse existing versions first and list gaps separately. Keep the tear on the left sleeve of the father's old coat and preserve the clock's position. Do not generate paid images or select new assets yet.

Check the scope first. Has the Agent invented unnecessary people or turned every passing description into a required prop? Can existing assets answer the need?

Matching names do not prove that two characters are the same. Similar titles do not prove a shared location. Link material to specific objects and revisions in the work.

## Separate identity, costume and story illustrations

Use a solo image or front view to identify a character. A costume reference describes what they wear in a scene; a story illustration shows what is happening. These can share a character while serving different purposes.

| Asset | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Character image | Face, apparent age, hair and identifying features |
| Costume | Garments, color, material and wearing condition |
| Location | Spatial relationships, entrances, light and key furnishings |
| Prop | Shape, scale, damage and actual use |
| Story illustration | Relationships, action and the linked chapter or scene |

Request side, back, turnaround or expression views for a specific need. Keep the same person and costume across views. A group illustration is not a substitute for an identity image.

## Reuse before filling gaps

Confirm the versions used throughout the episode. A chosen coat should not silently change because a new candidate looks more appealing.

For a new look, ask the Agent to explain the differences and which shots need review. Wet clothes after rain can be a story-state change without creating a new character identity in every shot.

See [Images and illustrations](illustrations.md) for preparing images and reviewing candidates.

## Judge the actual results

A correct reference does not guarantee that the prose, image or video follows it. If the left sleeve is torn, check whether the script wraps the right arm, the image is mirrored or the damage disappears after a cut.

Follow a character's appearances or review scene by scene. You can pause, enlarge and comment anywhere, not only on the Agent's summary or selected sample pictures.

## Keep old references when material changes

If the watch shop moves from the street to a courtyard, review entrances, windows, arrivals and sound sources. Ask for linked uses, then decide which need changes.

New reference material does not automatically validate old pictures. Preserve the images, scripts and feedback with their revisions. Read [Review reference changes](references.md).

Before a handoff, check original files, sources and permission to transfer them; see [Formats and delivery](formats-and-delivery.md).
