# Your creative library

The creative library is where you enter your work. Novels, screenplays and videos are independent projects. Begin with any one of them and link related works when you need to adapt or produce something.

One novel can lead to several scripts. A script can have different video treatments. Each keeps its own content, revisions and author decisions; a link helps you trace the source.

## Begin with what you have

| What you have | Start with | What you return to review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A story idea or novel draft | A novel | Outlines, chapters and full prose |
| A conflict, original script or authorized source | A screenplay | Episodes, scenes, action and dialogue |
| A brief, storyboard or existing clips | A video | Previews, actual clips, sound and the finished cut |

Tell your Agent: “I only want an original short drama. Prepare the characters and first episode without creating a novel.” Existing footage can also become the starting point for a video, without empty upstream projects.

For a first session, read the [Quick start](getting-started.md). To compare starting points, see [Choose a project starting point](creative-paths.md).

## A work and its folder are different things

A work is the object you are creating. Its storage location is the local folder holding its files. Related works can share a workspace or live separately. Sharing a folder does not share every permission or story fact.

When preparing a new work, tell your Agent its type, name and preferred location. Review the proposal before execution. If it already exists, open that work and continue rather than recreating it to reconnect an assistant.

An application update or navigation change does not require rebuilding your projects. Keep the original folders and backups; see [Your work, versions and backups](data-and-backup.md).

## Links preserve a particular source version

Suppose chapter twelve becomes episode one. Ask the Agent to preserve the source chapter and revision used for this adaptation. Later edits to the novel should not obscure what the script was based on.

> Try adapting this chapter into the first episode. Keep its source version and character relationships. Identify new dialogue as adaptation choices. Leave the novel unchanged and return a complete script for me to read.

A link does not automatically propagate novel changes into a script or write new adaptation details back into the original. Compare source changes before deciding what to update.

For linked reference changes, read [Review reference changes](references.md).

## Review in the right workspace

Read a novel as connected prose, a script as full action and dialogue, and a video with continuous picture and sound. Plans and summaries help you navigate; they do not replace the work itself.

Before giving feedback, check the work and revision you are looking at. Reviewing a reference draft, adopting it into a manuscript and finalizing a revision are separate decisions. Preview approval, clip selection and final video confirmation are separate too.

You can also edit directly. Working with an Agent does not require a generation task for every creative action.

## State the scope across related works

Permission to work on a novel does not automatically include its linked scripts or videos. Specify the objects the Agent may read and the content it may change.

“Refer to the novel's relationships and adjust episode one” has a different scope from “rewrite the novel chapter too.” Clear limits preserve your earlier decisions and make revisions easier to compare.
