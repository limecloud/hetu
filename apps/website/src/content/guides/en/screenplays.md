# Develop and review a screenplay

## Start with what you need

Start with a character, scene or existing episode. Trying a scene needs no novel or complete series plan. Save a draft when you want to continue it and keep deliberate adaptation choices explicit.

An original script can begin with a conflict. An adaptation begins with material you have permission to use. A screenplay has its own place in the creative library, with episodes, text, revisions and author decisions.

## Tell your Agent what the scene needs to do

Try a request like this:

> Develop an original short drama about a lighthouse keeper who hears her missing brother on a broken radio. Discuss the series direction, then write a complete first episode. Focus on their relationship. Do not create a novel or generate video yet.

Let your Agent organize character goals, conflict, episodes and open questions. Discuss the direction and choose what fits; the Agent saves the proposal without asking you to retype it into forms. Let the story shape episodes rather than a video model’s maximum clip length.

For existing work, continue the same script project. For an adaptation, fix the authorized source edition and revision first; see [From novel to screenplay](adaptation.md).

## Read a complete episode

Read through before inspecting individual details. An episode plan describes what might happen; a readable screenplay needs scene headings, action, characters and dialogue. A short outline is a different result from a finished draft.

Ask what the character wants on entering, what prevents it and what changes by the end. For adaptations, compare the source: which details are retained, compressed, reordered or added?

Reference drafts and author text remain separate. Read an available reference even when the author draft is empty, then decide what to adopt. Direct text editing remains available whenever you want it.

## Point to a place and continue the same episode

For example:

> Episode one reveals the brother’s secret too early. Keep the radio voice and her hesitation, but remove the explanatory dialogue. Revise only this scene and show me the differences first.

Attach feedback to the passage in Hetu, or return the exact draft and comment to the original Agent conversation. Keep the version and task together. Saving feedback and receiving a revised draft are separate events.

Compare the new version with the old one. Follow a character’s appearances or inspect the changes, then read the whole scene again. Feedback on the earlier revision stays attached to that revision.

## Choose text, finalize it and prepare production separately

Adopt a line or scene, keep editing and confirm a script revision when ready. Approving a preview or selecting a character image is a separate decision. It does not finalize the script or rewrite the source novel.
