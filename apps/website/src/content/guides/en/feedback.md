# Give feedback and revise

“She would not say this” and “this pause feels too short” are useful judgments. Point to the exact result and location, explain what to preserve and what to change, then continue the same task.

You can comment anywhere, not only on issues the assistant has marked.

## Read or watch the full result first

Read connected prose for a novel, full action and dialogue for a screenplay, and continuous picture with sound for a video. Summaries help you navigate; return to the context to judge.

Check the revision you are reviewing. If a newer result has arrived, old feedback still refers to the old version. Compare before asking for changes to the wrong passage.

## Give a location, concern, constraint and scope

> In the doorway scene of episode one, the daughter explains the clock's power too directly and removes the suspense. Keep her reaching out and withdrawing her hand. Rewrite only the two explanatory lines without changing other scenes.

Technical terminology is optional. You can also say, “I do not know how to fix this, but they should not reconcile so quickly.” Ask the Agent to propose two options.

| Result | How to identify the location | How to check the revision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Text | Chapter, scene or selected passage | Compare changes, then read the surrounding prose |
| Image | The image and specific region | Enlarge the detail, then review identity and composition |
| Video | A time range in the current version | Play before and after the change with sound |

## Save feedback and continue the original task

In the Mac task, choose the result you are reviewing and save your revision feedback. Copy the continuation prompt into the original Agent conversation. Have it read the feedback and fixed version before revising.

Hosts with a continuation-message feature handle the request according to their actual capabilities. Otherwise, copying the prompt works too. You do not need to recreate the work, import it again or reconfigure the connection.

Feedback saved, request received and new result returned are different states. If no revision appears, inspect the original task instead of treating an acknowledgment as completion.

## Compare the new result against your request

Check whether the concern was resolved and whether protected content stayed intact. A request to alter two lines should not also change a character's history or the ending.

Compare candidates independently. You can adopt part of one or retain them all as references. Do not accept an unsuitable version just to mark a task complete.

To make your own edits, continue in the author draft. Review, adoption and finalization are separate; see [Checks and draft review](review.md).

## Preserve continuity in video revisions

For example: “Extend the pause starting at eight seconds by half a beat. Let us hear footsteps inside before cutting to the doorway. Keep the other clips.” Have the Agent fix the range and identify effects on audio, subtitles and later timings.

Watch the entry and exit of the revision, dialogue, subtitles and overall rhythm. When duration changes, old time coordinates remain tied to the old cut.

Story concerns return to the script, identity concerns to assets, and pacing or sound concerns to editing. Review previews and actual final footage separately; see [From preview to finished video](video.md).

When an outcome is unknown, query the original operation before another paid generation. See [Troubleshooting and recovery](troubleshooting.md).
