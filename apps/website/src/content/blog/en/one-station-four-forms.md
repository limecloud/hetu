The same station can appear on stage, in a comedy bit, in an explanation or in a quest. Each form needs different structures to remain useful after a revision.

## Give stage cues explicit prerequisites

[Stage Rehearsal](/en/plugins/stage-rehearsal/) keeps the light and approaching train sound distinct, each with its trigger, department and prerequisites. Read a complete prompt book, compare holds and preserve the rehearsal basis. Changed dialogue leaves earlier cues and notes available for review.

## Let callbacks return to something already said

[Stand-up Studio](/en/plugins/standup-studio/) keeps material, setups, punchlines and tags. Alternative sets can use the same bits while exposing a callback that has not been seeded. A local punchline revision preserves other writing and earlier notes.

## Review numbers alongside explanations

[Science Writing Studio](/en/plugins/science-studio/) brings quantities and units into exact arithmetic. Twenty metres in ten seconds gives two metres per second, but an average does not describe every instant. Keep an analogy’s limits visible and revisit claims and paragraphs when data changes.

## Give a choice its actual cost

[Quest Studio](/en/plugins/quest-studio/) represents taking a letter, spending a ticket and entering the station as explicit state. Without the letter, a failed transition leaves the ticket intact. Replay a route with each before-and-after state, and preserve uncertainty when exploration is incomplete.

Tell your Agent the goal, return to inspect the complete result and name what needs revision. Each professional project retains its own evidence while informing the same work. Authors retain the choices that shape the expression.


## Use only what this work needs

Choose tools for a concrete problem in the work. Try an expression in conversation first; add an extension when professional structures, comparisons or delivery need it. Ordinary manuscripts and selected exports should remain retrievable.
