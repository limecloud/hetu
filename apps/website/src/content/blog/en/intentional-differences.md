# That contradiction may be the story

Chapter three says the father is dead. In chapter seven someone claims to have seen him last night. Software can flag the difference, but it cannot conclude that either line must be removed.

The witness may lie, the protagonist may be mistaken, or the author may be trying another direction.

## Different kinds of material need different treatment

Real-world research needs sources. Confirmed fictional facts follow the author's choices. Alternatives may contradict one another. Deliberate differences need narrative context. Turning every source into canon, or correcting every inconsistency, would limit the work.

Checks should state what was read, the evidence found and what remains unknown. Scores cannot decide which version is more moving.

## An adaptation can make a new choice

The mother delivers the letter in the novel; the sister delivers it in the script. Keep the source revision and the adaptation's decision without silently rewriting the original.

When a source changes, revisit its explicit dependencies. Whether and how to revise follows the creative goal; changes do not propagate automatically.

## Delegate execution while retaining judgment

An Agent can organize, draft and revise continuously within your chosen scope. Replacing your manuscript, confirming canon or selecting a final cut refers to a concrete result. A decision already expressed can be executed directly; successful generation does not approve everything on your behalf.

Read [maintaining a story bible](/en/docs/story-bible/) to record what is settled. To try two directions, a conversation can be enough.
