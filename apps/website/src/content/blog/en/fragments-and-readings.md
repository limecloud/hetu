# The light is still on. What comes next?

“After the rain, the light was still on.” It could open a personal essay or raise a question for a critical response: does the light suggest waiting, or illuminate a departure already under way?

## Keep the fragments available

[Essay Studio](/en/plugins/essay-studio/) keeps the rain, a remembered letter and an explicitly invented person distinct. Ask your Agent to arrange the same fragments twice: enter through the rain or begin with departure. Read both versions before naming a pause or recurring image to revisit.

A fragment can appear again at the end with its own position. Unused writing stays available. When one fragment changes, earlier arrangements have a clear reason to be reread.

## Make room for another reading

[Literary Criticism](/en/plugins/criticism-studio/) distinguishes the primary quotation, secondary commentary and your interpretation. A continuing light may suggest waiting; putting a letter back may suggest departure. The project retains reasons and scope while the author judges the argument.

Exact quotation is a beginning. A revised opening may need new claims; a changed interpretation may require another look at the paragraphs that use it. Competing readings can coexist without a score deciding the discussion.

## Continue from a specific passage

Tell your Agent the goal, return to read the complete work, then name a fragment or interpretation to revise. Continue from the feedback and its evidence. The essay and response remain distinct works that can inform one another.
