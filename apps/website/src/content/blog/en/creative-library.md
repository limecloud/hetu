## “I have a scene. Why do I need to create a book?”

A daughter wants the old clock. Her father has hidden the key. You want to write that argument, without a novel or an adaptation source.

Another creator has three clips of a confrontation at a doorway and wants to explore the edit. Asking for a novel, a world bible and an empty screenplay before those clips can be used creates more work.

Novels, scripts and videos begin in different places. A creative library helps you find and continue them. Once inside a work, the workspace should suit what you need to read or watch.

## Read chapters, follow scenes, watch picture and sound

A novelist needs continuous prose, chapters and story facts. A screenwriter needs to follow action, dialogue and the way scenes change the situation. A video creator needs to watch continuously, hear sound and inspect cuts and subtitles.

These activities can connect without sharing a single manuscript screen. Open an original script or continue existing footage directly, without inventing a production history that never happened.

Manual writing and editing remain available. For supporting work, give your external Agent the goal, let it organize the material and return to review the result.

## A connection needs a particular source version

A novel can support several adaptations. A script can support several video projects. Each has choices worth preserving.

Suppose a new chapter moves an argument to the following morning. Should the script change too? Perhaps, but the screenwriter may already have compressed it into a dinner scene. Someone needs to read the work and decide.

Source links therefore retain the version actually used. A novel revision invites an impact review; a script revision invites a shot review. Neither silently replaces downstream work that has already been made.

## Keeping works together does not share every permission

Asking an Agent to revise episode one does not authorize the entire creative library. Keeping a novel and script in one folder does not permit redistributing the source to every production partner.

Scope follows the work and task. Adaptation rights, permission to send material to a generation service and permission to include it in a handoff need separate checks. The creator avoids duplicate data entry while retaining meaningful decisions.

## Start from what you already have

Say “Develop an original episode without creating a novel,” or “Prepare a preview from the clips I have; do not generate new picture yet.” Your Agent organizes a proposal and returns the work. Read the complete script or watch the edit, then point to the change you want.

A story may move between forms or stay in the one you prefer. The creative library keeps completed work understandable, so the next revision has somewhere to begin.
